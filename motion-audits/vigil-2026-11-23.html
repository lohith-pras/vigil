Motion audit · design-motion-principles

vigil — a beautiful instrument that moves like a screensaver

Vigil is a Windows tray panel (WPF, .NET 10) for laptop health: CPU, RAM, disks, network, battery, keep-awake, displays. You open it many times a day for a one-second glance. This audit reads the motion through that frequency.

Overall

The engineering of the motion is excellent: one shared curve, a single gate for reduced motion that keeps fades and drops movement, loops that only run while visible, and animations that retarget instead of restarting. The problem is tempo. Almost everything runs 500–1400 ms on a slow-start curve, on a panel opened for a glance. Fully settling after open takes about a second, a tab change about 1.2 s, and live readings are mid-animation most of the time. Keep the craft and the restraint of the design doc; cut every duration to a third and drop the stagger.

Emil Kowalski — Restraint & Speed

Primary

Problem. The motion you chose is purposeful; the tempo is wrong for a glance-and-go panel.

What's working well
  • Close is instant (Hide() on Esc and on deactivate). Exactly right for a flyout. PanelWindow.xaml.cs:52
  • Press feedback: scale .97, 140 ms, strong exponential ease-out on every button, chip and tab. Textbook. PanelWindow.xaml:48
  • Switch knob 200 ms, track colour 180 ms. In budget.
  • BeginAnimation from the current value retargets mid-flight instead of restarting, and the traffic lanes change speed through SpeedRatio so they never restart.
  • No bounce anywhere; distances are 4–6 px.
Issues to address
CriticalEmilJakub

Every open takes about a second to settle

WhatThe shell fades and rises over 400 ms, then each section of the page fades and rises over 700 ms, 70 ms apart. A page with five sections finishes at about 1 s. This runs on every open, and the same stagger runs again on every tab change.

Why it mattersThe panel is opened tens of times a day to read one number. The reading you came for is at partial opacity for most of a second. Two lists with stagger in the same view is also the stagger-spam tell.

Recommended motionShell: opacity 0→1 plus 4 px rise, 160 ms, strong ease-out. Drop RevealSections entirely; content arrives with the shell.

Tab change is slow and animates from the keyboard too

WhatThe cobalt dot travels 550 ms and stretches to 2.4× wide at the halfway point; the page slides 5 px over 500 ms, then the section stagger runs again. Tab_Checked plays all of it whether the tab was clicked or chosen with the arrow keys.

Why it mattersTabs are the most frequent action inside the panel. Keyboard-initiated changes should never animate, and a 2.4× stretch makes the dot itself the thing you notice.

Recommended motionDot: translate only, 200 ms strong ease-out (stretch at most 1.3×, or none). Page: 150 ms opacity crossfade, no slide, no stagger. Keyboard: set positions directly (check InputManager.Current.MostRecentInputDevice is KeyboardDevice).

Live readings and bars are always mid-animation

WhatValues sample every 1 s. Changed digits rise 30 % of a line and fade in over 500 ms; bars ease over 1.4 s. Busy readings are animating half the time and bars never reach their value before the next one arrives.

Why it mattersAn instrument should be readable at a glance. A bar that is always gliding shows a value from up to a second ago, and moving digits are harder to read than still ones.

Recommended motionDigits: 180 ms, 15 % of a line, strong ease-out. Bars: 300 ms strong ease-out (keep "first value after showing is set directly"). RAM colour change: 200 ms.

ImportantEmil

One slow-start curve for everything, including enters

WhatMotion.Soft is cubic-bezier(.45,.05,.25,1), an ease-in-out whose first control point (x .45, y .05) makes the first ~30 % of every animation almost stationary. It drives enters, feedback and on-screen moves alike.

Why it mattersA slow start is exactly the moment the eye is watching. Enters and feedback on this curve feel late even before you shorten them; at 500–700 ms the effect doubles.

Recommended motionAdd Motion.Out = (.23,1,.32,1) for enters, feedback, readings and bars. Keep Soft only for things travelling on screen (dot-field slide, rows re-ranking). Update DESIGN.md's "one curve" rule to two.

Feedback that should be instant takes most of a second

WhatSelecting a chip spreads the white fill from the click point over 750 ms; arming End fades to red over 450 ms.

Why it mattersOrigin-aware fill is a great idea, but it is a response to a click. At 750 ms the user's eye has already moved on before the chip says "selected". The armed red state is a warning and must read immediately.

Opportunities
  • Under Windows "Show animations" off, the panel open still fades 400 ms (fades are kept). Fine in principle; at 160 ms it becomes a non-issue. Motion.cs:16
  • Dot field: 950 ms slide per 1 s sample means the field is permanently moving. Consider 300 ms, or only the new column lighting (no slide). DotMatrix.cs:21

Through Emil's lens: the restraint is real (instant close, no bounce, tiny distances, a reduced-motion gate), but restraint also means speed. Divide every duration by about three and the panel will feel as fast as it actually is.

Jakub Krehel — Production Polish

Secondary

Mixed. Quiet and consistent; a few spots draw attention to themselves or snap.

What's working well
  • Reduced motion is handled properly: Motion.Enabled follows Windows "Show animations", movement and loops stop, comprehension fades stay. Better than most web apps. Motion.cs:13–17
  • Loops run only while the panel is visible and their element is on screen (SetAmbient), which also protects the 1 % CPU budget.
  • Only digits that changed move (RollingText), and all numbers are Geist Mono, so nothing shifts width.
  • Bars set the first value directly after showing, so nothing sweeps from stale data.
  • Motion.Off for the self-test gives deterministic screenshots.
Issues to address
ImportantJakubEmil

Breathing ring on the awake dot (plus a breathing colon)

WhatWhile keep-awake holds, a ring around the green header dot scales 1→2.6× and fades, forever, every 4 s. On the Keep awake tab the countdown colon also fades to 45 % and back every 2 s.

Why it mattersA looping pulse on a status dot is the most recognisable AI-slop motion pattern. The dot already says "awake"; the loop adds attention, not information, and two loops on one page compete.

Recommended motionPlay one ring (≈600 ms, ease-out) when keep-awake starts, then keep the dot still. Make the colon static, or tick it once per second (a clock does, a breath doesn't).

Exits as long as enters; re-rank glide is slow

WhatAn ended app's row fades out and slides 6 px left over 500 ms, the same weight as a row arriving. Rows gliding to a new rank take 800 ms, every 2 s sample.

Why it mattersThe user is moving on after End; the exit should get out of the way. An 800 ms glide on a 2 s refresh means the list is in motion 40 % of the time, which makes it hard to aim at the next row.

Recommended motionExit: 200 ms, 4 px, ease-out. Re-rank glide: 300 ms on Soft. New rows: 200 ms fade.

ImportantJakub

Disclosures snap open (Options, Alerts, Until…)

WhatOptions, the Alerts block and the Until row switch Visibility directly, and the chevron swaps glyph (› to ⌄). Everything below jumps down in one frame. These are the only unanimated state changes in a panel where everything else moves.

Why it mattersIn this panel the contrast makes them feel broken rather than snappy. A short fade lets the eye follow what appeared.

Recommended motionContent: opacity 0→1 plus 4 px drop, 180 ms ease-out (no height animation needed in WPF). Chevron: one glyph rotated 90°, 180 ms. Close: instant or 120 ms fade.

Opportunities
  • Hover washes (#0AFFFFFF) on tabs, chips and links switch instantly. A 120 ms colour transition would match the rest of the panel's softness. PanelWindow.xaml:144, 235, 297

Jhey Tompkins — Experimentation & Delight

Selective

Strong where it counts. The character comes from data, not decoration.

What's working well
  • The wordmark's i-dot is an eye that blinks every 10 s, and every 6 s while holding the PC awake: brand, name and state in one tiny motion. Keep it.
  • Traffic dots fall for download and climb for upload, speed following the rate and hidden when idle. Motion as data.
  • The End drain line shows the 3 s arming window without a number.
Opportunities
  • Keep-awake starting is a rare, meaningful moment: let the eye "open" once (scaleY .15→1, 300 ms) instead of looping the header ring.
  • Battery-over-time (proposed for v0.4) could draw its trend line once on first view with a clip-path reveal, then stay still.
  • Charging: a single slow fill sweep on the battery bar when the charger is plugged in (event-driven, not looped).

Through Jhey's lens: Vigil's personality lives in tiny, meaningful motions (eye, traffic, drain). Spend delight on rare state changes such as plugging in or starting keep-awake, never on things that repeat every second.

07 · Lens reference summary

Which lens did the work

Emil drove 5 of 8 findings: frequency and speed are the issue, not taste.

Jakub supplied the reduced-motion and exit judgements and confirmed the strongest parts (gating, stable numerals, quiet distances).

Jhey mostly endorsed what exists; his suggestions move delight from loops to rare events.

To lean differently: making Jakub primary would turn findings 1, 2 and 4 into Important rather than Critical (polish lens tolerates 300–500 ms), but the stagger and the breathing ring would still be flagged.
